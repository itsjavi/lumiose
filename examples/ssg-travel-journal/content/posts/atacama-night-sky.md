The Atacama is so dry that the landscape looks unfinished: salt flats, red rock, volcanoes standing
on the horizon like a row of cones. By day it is hot and white. By night it becomes the best
planetarium on the planet.

## Altitude first

Much of the region sits well above two thousand metres, and some of the famous viewpoints are far
higher. I spent the first two days doing very little on purpose:

- Drink more water than seems reasonable.
- Skip the high geysers until day three or four.
- Walk slowly. Then walk more slowly.

## The sky

On a moonless night the Milky Way doesn't look like a smudge here; it looks like a structure, with
dark lanes of dust and bright clouds of stars. Our guide pointed out the two Magellanic Clouds,
small galaxies visible to the naked eye, and I spent the rest of the night checking they were still
there.

> I'd always thought of the sky as a ceiling. In the Atacama it felt like a window.

Bring your warmest layers. The desert gives back its heat to the sky very quickly, and by four in
the morning the stars were the only warm-looking thing around.

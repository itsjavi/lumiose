There are eighteen islands in the Faroes and I saw perhaps four of them properly. The rest were
suggestions: a green shoulder rising out of white, a lighthouse that appeared for ten seconds and
went back into hiding, sheep standing on slopes that should not hold sheep.

## Walking in the white

Fog changes how you hike. You stop looking at the view and start looking at the path: the cairns,
the wet grass, the line where the cliff simply ends. Every walk here has a moment where the ground
in front of you becomes sky.

1. Check the local trail notes before you go; some routes cross private land and ask for a small fee.
2. Carry a real map. The fog does not care about your phone signal.
3. Turn back early. The cliffs are unfenced and very, very tall.

## Small towns, long evenings

The villages are tiny and turf-roofed, with churches painted black and window frames painted every
other colour. In August the light lasts until almost midnight, so dinner happens late and slowly,
usually with a view of a harbour that may or may not be visible.

On the last morning the fog lifted for an hour. I didn't take a single photograph. I just sat on
the harbour wall and looked.

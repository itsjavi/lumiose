Kyoto in autumn is famous, which means Kyoto in autumn is busy. The trick, it turns out, is
embarrassingly simple: get up before everyone else. At six in the morning the temple paths are
quiet, the moss is wet and the maples look like they've been lit from inside.

## A sunrise route

1. A shrine with a tunnel of gates on the hillside, walked before the first train arrives.
2. Breakfast near the station: rice, miso soup, grilled fish, pickles.
3. A temple garden while the gardeners are still sweeping leaves off the gravel.
4. A nap. This is important.

## Eating slowly

The best meal of the trip was in a small place with eight counter seats, where the cook made each
course in front of us and explained, with careful English and a lot of pointing, which mountain the
mushrooms came from.

- Many small restaurants take bookings only by phone or through your hotel; ask early.
- Department store food halls are a perfect, cheap lunch.
- Tea houses close earlier than you'd expect.

By nine the crowds arrived, and I was already sitting by the river with a coffee, very pleased with
myself.

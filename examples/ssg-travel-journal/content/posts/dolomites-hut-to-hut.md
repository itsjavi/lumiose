The plan was simple: walk from one _rifugio_ to the next, carry as little as possible and let
someone else cook. The Dolomites make this easy. Huts sit on saddles and ledges a day's walk apart,
and each one has a hot meal, a dormitory and a terrace that faces something absurdly beautiful.

## The stages

| Day | From → to             | Distance | Climb  |
| --- | --------------------- | -------- | ------ |
| 1   | Valley → first saddle | 11 km    | 950 m  |
| 2   | Saddle → plateau hut  | 14 km    | 700 m  |
| 3   | Plateau → lake hut    | 9 km     | 550 m  |
| 4   | Lake → ridge hut      | 12 km    | 1050 m |
| 5   | Ridge → valley        | 15 km    | 200 m  |

## Notes for next time

- Book the huts early. The good ones fill months ahead.
- Bring a silk sleeping-bag liner; blankets are provided, sheets are not.
- Start at seven. Afternoon storms are a regular appointment in summer.
- Pack a headlamp for the dormitory, and the patience of a saint for the snoring.

The fourth day was the hardest and the best: a long climb on scree, a narrow ledge with a cable to
hold, and then the hut on the ridge, glowing in the evening light with the whole range below it.
